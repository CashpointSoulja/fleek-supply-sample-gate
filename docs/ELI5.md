# ELI5

Imagine you run a shop and want to start selling a new kind of jacket because your customers keep asking for it. A new seller says, "I have thousands of jackets, here are a few to look at."

Before you buy a big pile, you check the few they sent:
- **Did you look at enough of them?** Looking at 3 jackets tells you almost nothing.
- **How many were good?** And how sure can you be, given how many you looked at?
- **Are there photos of each one?** If not, you can't check later.
- **Are they the jackets they promised?** Or did they slip in other stuff?
- **Do they actually have enough to keep selling to you?**
- **Is the price sensible?**
- **Do your customers really want these, at this price?**

If something is clearly wrong, you say **no**. If you don't know enough yet, you say **show me more**. If everything checks out, you say **let's try a small batch**, and you decide in advance how small.

This app is that checklist, with the maths done for you and a written record of what you decided and why. It never buys anything and never messages anyone. A "small batch" here is just a recommendation written in a log.
